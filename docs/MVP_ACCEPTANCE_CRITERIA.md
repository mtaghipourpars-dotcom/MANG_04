# MVP Acceptance Criteria

## Objective
Prove that one issue affecting a key resource can move through an evidence-based, human-authorized, traceable decision lifecycle to an outcome review.

## Required demonstration
1. Register an issue with minimal required information and identify the affected key resource and its unique responsible owner.
2. Attach or reference evidence and explicitly label unknown, assumed, estimated, incomplete, stale, or contradictory data.
3. Qualify the issue without silently discarding it; record the qualification result and rationale.
4. Enrich the issue progressively with stakeholder, commitment, resource, and impact information as applicable.
5. Identify relevant solution candidates using the issue type and applicability rules.
6. Assess candidate feasibility and expected impact using a versioned, explainable profile. Do not invent quantitative results when inputs or formulas are missing.
7. Show that a solution may be a combination of actions or resource allocations rather than a project approval/rejection.
8. Respect authority boundaries and route decisions outside local delegated authority to the appropriate higher level.
9. Record decision-maker, authority, selected solution(s), rejected alternatives and reasons, evidence snapshot, profile version, and decision time.
10. Track actions, accountable owners, dates, status, and execution evidence.
11. Compare expected and observed outcomes; distinguish effectiveness from resource efficiency and preserve uncertainty about causal attribution.
12. Preserve historical decisions and their original inputs/profile versions even after later profile changes.
13. Enforce role-based access and issue confidentiality as distinct controls.
14. Label demonstration, assumed, simulated, and live data accurately.

## MVP exclusions unless separately implemented and validated
- Autonomous decisions or automatic overruling of management.
- Advanced machine learning or self-optimizing decision weights.
- Live ERP/SCADA/finance integration.
- Guaranteed detection of deliberate data manipulation.
- Precise causal attribution of financial outcomes without defensible methodology.
- Organization-wide optimization presented as mathematically optimal when constraints/data are incomplete.

## Acceptance evidence
A reviewer should be able to follow a single issue from registration to outcome and answer: what was known, what was uncertain, which options were considered, why the human decision was made, what resources/actions were committed, what happened, and what can legitimately be learned.
